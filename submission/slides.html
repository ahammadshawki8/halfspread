The problem
03 / 15

Entry and exit cost the same at any one instant. So "the exit is expensive" is a claim about time.

We re-quoted the same contracts every few minutes for a full session to test it. Below is a position the desk actually held, priced at the opening bell and again near the close.

SPY 765 put, at the open
1.235%

2.857%
bid 0.17 / ask 0.18, on a mid of 0.175
cost to leave, relative
2.31x
The gap held at a penny while the option decayed, so the same penny became twice the price.

Why it compounds

Two forces push the same way: dealers widen quotes as gamma risk rises into the close, and the contract itself decays. A fixed spread on a shrinking premium is a rising percentage.

Who pays it

Every agent that opens a position in the morning and closes it in the afternoon. That is the standard design, and it pays the widest spread of the day on the way out.

The way out of it

An out-of-the-money contract that reaches expiry is removed at zero. No closing trade exists, so no spread is crossed and no commission is charged.

89 observations of this contract are in the journal, timestamped, and the chart on the live site is drawn from them. The exit is where the money dies, and it dies on a clock.

The solution
04 / 15

Build the agent backwards from the cost, and the exit disappears.

HALFSPREAD sells defined-risk option spreads and chooses strikes with one objective: that the contracts expire worthless. The structure is ordinary. The selection criterion and the intent to hold are not.

Chain, quotes and underlying across SPY, XSP and SPXW every cycle
2 · Price
Own Black-Scholes, own greeks, own implied volatility from the mid
3 · Measure cost

4 · Net EV
Rank on value after that cost, not on a delta target
5 · Gate
Win probability, loss caps, daily budget, concurrency

Research
05 / 15

A leading 2026 study was corrected last month for exactly this error. Every result flipped.

Vilkov's 0DTE trading-rules paper charged the bid-ask half-spread at one hundredth of its true size, 0.022 basis points instead of 2.2. External replicators found it. The corrected net Sharpe ratios:

No strategy or basket retains a positive net Sharpe ratio.

The author's own conclusion, after the correction.
  • We do not claim his numbers support us. His structures are near the money and round-tripped at 16:00. Ours are further out and held to settlement. Different regime, separate evidence.
  • We cite him for the cost finding, which is the part that generalises: mis-price execution and every conclusion downstream is wrong.
  • The annotated paper in that repository still shows pre-correction figures. Only the issues file is current.
Supporting evidence we do rely on: a 40-delta 7DTE bull put spread held to expiration ran an 80% win rate from 2023 to mid-2026, and Alpaca charges no commission on contracts that simply expire.

Where we actually differ
  • Exit-cost avoidance as the design principle, not a cost model bolted onto a signal. One rival states it closes every position before expiry, which is the literal anti-pattern.
  • The Vilkov correction. A reversal of a leading paper, caused by precisely the error the field is making. No other entry has it.

  • The CLI as the execution path. Most entries used MCP or the raw SDK. Every order we place is a replayable command.
The strategies that Vilkov's correction invalidated are the ones roughly a dozen entries here are still running. Being right about cost is not a nice detail. It decides whether the rest of the work counts.

Architecture
07 / 15

A deterministic core, a narrow AI, and a journal that everything is derived from.

Decision layer
  • pricing.py Black-Scholes, implied volatility by bisection, greeks
  • chain.py OCC parsing, chain fetch, put-call parity forward
  • cost.py half-spread measurement, net EV, admissibility
  • strategy.py candidate construction and ranking
  • risk.py shortfall sizing, loss caps, concurrency
Runtime
  • execute.py mleg build, re-quote, submit through the CLI
  • monitor.py net-of-cost exit test, emergency close
  • settle.py expiry accounting and the counterfactual
  • llm.py bounded event-risk review, clamped in code
  • session.py one entrypoint, local or GitHub Actions
Evidence
  • journal.py append-only JSONL, fsynced, one line per decision including refusals
  • verify.py re-derives every published claim with no key and no network
  • publish.py builds the dashboard payload from the journal alone
  • mcp_readonly.py stdio proxy that strips mutating tools

Zero dependencies

GitHub Actions is the runtime

A scheduled job runs a session and commits its own journal, so the audit trail is timestamped by git rather than by the agent.

GitHub Pages is the front end

The page computes nothing. It renders a payload derived from the journal, so it cannot drift from the record.

No card, no paid tier

Free Alpaca data, free Groq inference, free hosting. Every constraint in this project was solved without spending anything.

Technology
08 / 15

The free data feed gives no greeks and no spot price. So we compute the pricing layer ourselves.

This began as a blocker and became the most defensible part of the build. Strike selection rests on mathematics we own and can show, rather than on a vendor field we would have to trust.

Greeks are all zero

Every contract at every expiry returns delta, gamma, theta and vega of exactly 0, with implied volatility null. Not a market-hours artifact.

Solved: implied volatility recovered from the mid by bisection, then delta, gamma, theta and vega derived from it.

No spot for cash indices

Alpaca publishes no underlying price for XSP or SPXW, so moneyness cannot be computed the obvious way.

Solved: the forward is recovered from put-call parity on the chain itself, using calls and puts at a shared strike.

Real markets are not lognormal

Black-Scholes understates the left tail, which is precisely the tail a put seller is exposed to.

Solved: breach frequency counted from 5,539 hourly bars across 668 sessions, bucketed by hour. Sizing takes whichever estimate is worse.

The AI
10 / 15

There is a language model in here. It is allowed to do exactly one thing: make a position smaller.

Roughly thirty entries in this hackathon pitch "the LLM proposes and risk gates decide". That territory is saturated, and we think it is also the wrong shape. Our model has authority over one scalar.

Deterministic engine
Strike, width, size, limit price and exit are all arithmetic. Decided before the model is consulted, and unchanged if it never answers.
AI event review
Reads live headlines through Alpaca's news API. Returns one number.
Clamped in code
Forced into the range 0 to 1. A model that asks to double gets truncated to no change.
Final size
Scaled down, or unchanged. Never up.
The model cannot
  • Choose a strike, a width or an expiry
  • Enlarge a position by any amount
  • Overrule a risk gate or a loss cap
  • Abstain from trading entirely. A block is converted to a floor of 0.25 and logged as a conversion, because refusing a capped-loss trade over tail risk is the wrong response to a bounded tail.
What it earned its place doing
  • On its first live session it pulled Strait of Hormuz attacks and a crude oil surge out of the headlines and cut the position.
  • It independently flagged the payrolls print scheduled for the following morning.
  • Reading unstructured event risk off the wire is the one job in this pipeline a human is genuinely worse at automating.
  • If Groq is unreachable, the deterministic decision stands and the desk keeps trading.
Four vetoes are recorded in the journal with their reasoning and their resulting multiplier. Give the model less to do, and what it does can be trusted.

Alpaca integration
11 / 15

The CLI is the agent's hands. MCP is the window. Read-only is enforced, not promised.

Execution, through the CLI
  • Every broker action is a logged alpaca invocation, recorded verbatim in the journal and replayable by hand from the command line.
  • Multi-leg orders go out as order_class: mleg with both legs covered inside the same order, which is what makes them Level 3 legal with no naked short.
  • A finding worth the deck space: Alpaca signs an mleg limit price from the package's point of view. Positive is a maximum debit, negative is a minimum credit. A credit spread submitted positive silently behaves as a market order. We proved it three ways and the sign is now forced in code.
  • Decision-to-execution drift measured at $7 per contract against a $1 bid-ask, so every order re-reads both legs seconds before submission and journals the drift.
Inspection, through MCP
  • Alpaca's official MCP server, v3.4.7, wired into the project and verified over stdio.
  • The vendor's own toolset filter does not remove place_option_order, so a configuration promise would have been worth nothing.
  • We wrote a stdio proxy that sits in front of it, strips mutating tools from the listing and refuses them by name on call.

Exit policy
12 / 15

Even the emergency exit is decided on net cost, never on a distance trigger.

The obvious rule is to close when the underlying approaches the short strike. For a defined-risk spread that rule is actively harmful, and it is worth explaining why, because it is where most agents lose money.

Why the obvious rule is wrong
  • The loss is already capped by the long leg. There is no tail to escape.
  • By the time a short strike is threatened, the exit spread has widened to several times entry cost. We measured 3.92x on SPY in a single session.
  • So the trigger pays the exact cost this agent exists to avoid, in order to escape a loss that was bounded from the beginning.
The test we use instead

What does buying the package back cost right now, against the expected terminal intrinsic if we simply hold? Close only when the market is charging materially less than we expect to pay at settlement.

Materiality is the greater of $25 or 25% of the exit cost, which stops the desk churning on modelled edge that sits inside the noise of our own volatility estimate.

It is the same net-of-cost test the entry uses, pointed the other way.

Holding is a decision too

When the desk holds through a breach it writes a hold_through_breach record with the full arithmetic, so a decision not to act is as visible as one to act.

Emergency close still exists

It is booked explicitly as a cost event rather than hidden in the P&L, so the ledger never flatters itself.

Used so far

Zero times. Every position resolved at settlement, which is what the strategy is for.

Assignment risk on an ITM SPY finish is real and disclosed. It only bites when the short strike is already breached, which is the one case the arithmetic above is written to handle.

Verification
14 / 15

Our dashboard once published $316 that never happened. Here is what we did about it.

The ledger showed $316 realised across six settled positions while every contract still had six hours to run. Two bugs: settlement compared only the expiry date, and journal records were not scoped by account, so DEV experiments were being attributed to the competition account.

What we did not do

Quietly delete the records and re-publish. That is the version nobody would have caught.

What we did

Removed the eleven fabricated records and appended a journal_correction in their place, so the removal is a permanent part of the audit trail rather than an absence in it.

What it produced

A verifier that re-derives every published claim from the committed journal, and regression tests for both bugs. Neither can reach production again without failing the build.
